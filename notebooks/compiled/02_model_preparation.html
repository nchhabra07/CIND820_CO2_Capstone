Forecasting design

The project predicts each country/entity’s Total fossil CO₂ emissions one year ahead. For predictor year t, the target is the same entity’s Total emissions at t+1. Target construction is performed within entity using grouped chronological shifting, and every usable pair is checked to have a one-year gap.

Predictors

The seven historical predictors are Total_lag1, Coal_lag1, Oil_lag1, Gas_lag1, Cement_lag1, Flaring_lag1, and Per_Capita_lag1. They represent year-t information relative to the t+1 target.

Persistence baseline

The baseline predicts next year’s Total emissions as equal to the current year’s Total. Initial validation performance was MAE 4.806, RMSE 21.715, and R² 0.9993.

Expanded validation and final evaluation

The notebook subsequently performs limited nonlinear tuning, efficiency checks, six chronological validation windows, champion selection, and a held-out final test. Linear Regression is selected from the broader temporal evidence, then retrained through 2017 for final testing.

Linear Regression improves aggregate MAE by about 6.53% and RMSE by about 20.36%, but persistence still has the smaller individual absolute error for 73.58% of test observations. The learned model’s aggregate advantage is therefore interpreted cautiously.

Robustness and interpretation

VIF analysis identifies severe multicollinearity when Total is combined with source components. Coefficients are therefore not treated as independent causal effects. Components-only and Total-only sensitivity checks, Ridge regularization, residual diagnostics, year-specific errors, and performance-by-emissions-scale analysis are included as robustness evidence.

Main limitation

Historical emissions variables cannot directly anticipate policy changes, economic shifts, energy-market changes, or abrupt external disruptions. The model is therefore a short-horizon statistical forecasting tool, not a causal policy model.
