Purpose

Initial structural and quality assessment of the Global Carbon Project GCB2022v27 fossil CO₂ dataset before preprocessing and modelling.

Missingness and historical structure

Total emissions are nearly complete (0.32% missing), while source-specific variables have much greater historical missingness. Missingness is concentrated in earlier periods and was therefore profiled by time rather than treated as random.

Zero values

Zero Total-emission observations are heavily concentrated in early history. Their share falls from about 65.5% across the full dataset to 10.2% from 1950 onward, 8.3% from 1960 onward, and 4.8% from 2000 onward. Zero values are retained as valid recorded observations rather than treated as missing.

1960 was selected because it provides a strong balance between core-variable completeness and retention of more than six decades of modelling history. Later start dates improve completeness only modestly while reducing training history.

Entity scope

Eight entities were flagged for special review because they lack standard ISO codes or represent aggregate/special-purpose series. These include the Global and International Transport series plus six non-standard historical/special entities.

Distribution and outliers

Total and source-specific emissions are strongly right-skewed. Extreme observations were not automatically deleted because many are legitimate high-emission countries/entities. Per-capita extremes can also occur for small-population entities.

Relationships

Absolute emissions variables are strongly correlated: Total with Coal ≈ 0.98, Oil ≈ 0.98, Gas ≈ 0.97, Cement ≈ 0.93, and Flaring ≈ 0.92. The log-transformed Total–Coal correlation remains strong at 0.849. Per Capita has a very weak raw linear correlation with absolute Total emissions (about 0.03), indicating a different emissions dimension.

Modelling implications

Same-year source components should not be used to predict same-year Total because they contribute directly to the target. The forecasting design therefore uses historical year-t information to predict Total at t+1, uses chronological validation, retains legitimate extreme values, and later checks multicollinearity explicitly.
